# M3b1 RTMP Outputs Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Serve every stream to RTMP players (ffmpeg, MediaMTX as a puller) from the same port that takes RTMP publishers, and push streams out to remote RTMP servers, on our own `pkg/rtmp`.

**Architecture:** `pkg/rtmp`'s session becomes safe for one reader goroutine and one writer, and gains the server side of play and the client side of publish. One RTMP listener per address (moved from `internal/ingest` to a neutral `internal/rtmpsrv`) routes publishers to ingest by publish path and players to the play output by stream name. `internal/mux/flv` turns hub frames into RTMP messages (sequence headers from `Config`, enhanced RTMP for H.265, per-connection millisecond timestamps). `internal/output/rtmpplay` serves players from the hub; `internal/output/rtmppush` pushes a stream to a remote server with reconnect. `internal/conform` reads `rtmp://` so `osconform` checks the RTMP output.

**Tech Stack:** Go (`go 1.26.11`); `Eyevinn/mp4ff` v0.52.0 (AVC/HEVC decoder configuration records; every SPS/VPS already passed M2c's `h26x` gate); standard library; `stretchr/testify`. Test peers: ffmpeg/ffprobe (CI apt 6.1), MediaMTX 1.21.3 (CI RTMP service container on host 19350; the host-network RTSP container from M2c).

**Spec:** [docs/REBUILD_PLAN.md](../../REBUILD_PLAN.md) §2 (Outputs: RTMP play, RTMP push-out), §3.1, §3.3, §3.4, §3.7 (`pkg/rtmp`), the M3 row and the M3b1 row of §5, "Out of M2b" (server play and client publish → M3b). References: Adobe RTMP 1.0 and FLV 10.1, Enhanced RTMP v1 — never another product's code.

**M3b1 exit criteria** (the M3 row restricted to RTMP; VLC-class clients are checked in M3b3 for every output): ffmpeg/ffprobe and MediaMTX (as a puller) play our RTMP output for H.264+AAC and H.265+AAC; our push-out is accepted by ffmpeg's RTMP listener and by MediaMTX; for the RTMP play output and the push-out, `osconform`/`ReadRTMP` passes the steady-state, epoch-change, slow-consumer and B-frame scenarios and ffprobe reads it; publishers and players share one port without exposing publish keys.

**Out of M3b1, with reasons** (ledger rulings at dispatch): RTMPS (TLS, M5); play authentication and tokens (M6); seek/pause and `receiveAudio`/`receiveVideo` (live only: acknowledged, ignored); AMF3; legacy H.265 with FLV codec ID 12 (non-standard; enhanced RTMP only); more than one target per push-out (configure several); a mid-session video or audio codec change (players and push targets cannot follow one: the play session ends and the push republishes, see Global Constraints); VLC-class client checks (M3b3).

## Global Constraints

- Every Global Constraint of the M1, M3a, M2a, M2b and M2c plans still holds (independent libraries; `pkg/...` never imports `internal/...`; errors `pkg: op: %w`; never log and return the same error; every goroutine has a cancel path; hub writes never block — outputs read through `hub.Sub` only; parameter sets only in `Config`; credentials and stream keys redacted; peer tests via `internal/testpeer` (or, in `pkg/...` tests, a package-local mirror of it such as `pkg/rtmp`'s `requireFFmpeg`); tests and CI never bind 554/1935/8554 nor touch other containers — a v1 soak runs on the dev machine).
- **Ownership:** every slice `pkg/rtmp` returns is a fresh allocation; `WriteMedia` does not retain the caller's payload after it returns.
- **Session concurrency (`pkg/rtmp`):** after a play is accepted or a publish started, exactly one reader goroutine (owned by the conn, stopped by `Close`) handles inbound messages (acks, pings, control, commands, `onStatus`) and any other goroutine may call `WriteMedia`; every outbound write (media and control) holds one write mutex, so control writes never interleave with a media message's chunks. `Config.WriteTimeout` (default 10 s) bounds each `WriteMedia`; play conns may override it with `SetWriteTimeout`. `Done() <-chan struct{}` closes and `Err()` reports the cause when the conn ends. A publishing client has no inbound idle timeout (servers send acks only once per window); its liveness comes from write deadlines and TCP keepalive.
- **Timestamps:** per connection, the first frame sent defines the base `b` = its DTS; a message timestamp is `uint32(floor((DTS−b)/90))` ms, so every connection starts near 0 and stays below 2^24 ms (no extended timestamps) for the first 4.6 h; after that it wraps naturally at 2^32. Video `CompositionTime = floor((PTS−b)/90) − floor((DTS−b)/90)` (never negative). Per track the timestamps never decrease (mod 2^32).
- **Data and headers per epoch:** at the connection start, after every `Resync`, and before the first frame of every new epoch, the muxer writes exactly one `onMetaData` (play: plain `onMetaData`; publish: prefixed with `@setDataFrame`) listing only the tracks carried — `width`, `height`, `framerate` if known, `videocodecid` 7 (H.264) or 1752589105 (`'hvc1'`, H.265), `audiocodecid` 10, `audiosamplerate`, `audiochannels`/`stereo` — then, for each carried track, its sequence header just before that track's first frame of the epoch (so a track whose new `Config` arrives later in the epoch gets the right header) (H.264 AVCDecoderConfigurationRecord with legacy codec ID 7; H.265 HEVCDecoderConfigurationRecord as enhanced `PacketTypeSequenceStart`, FourCC `hvc1`; AAC AudioSpecificConfig with `AACPacketType 0`), all with frame type key. A new `Config` pointer always comes with a new epoch (Clock rule), so this covers resolution changes.
- **Codec changes:** when an epoch changes a carried track's codec (H.264↔H.265, audio codec) or a kind (video or audio) appears that the connection did not carry, the play output sends `EndOfStream` and closes the player (players reconnect); the pusher closes and republishes (counted `republishes`). Resolution and parameter changes within a codec only resend headers.
- **Start and resume:** a connection whose first delivered frame is video starts at a video keyframe (the hub's gate gives that for a fresh `Sub`); one whose first delivered frame is audio is audio-only and starts on that frame (the hub releases audio first only when no live video track is gated), and video appearing later is "a kind appears" (`ErrCodecChanged`). For a connection carrying video, at the connection start and after `Delivery.Lost > 0` (`Resync`) the muxer drops frames until the next video keyframe, writes onMetaData and headers, and drops audio whose PTS is below that keyframe's PTS (counted `dropped_early_audio`); any frame with DTS below the connection base `b` is dropped and counted (`dropped_before_base`), so timestamps never go negative and every capture epoch starts aligned as the Clock does at epoch starts. Video NAL units are 4-byte length-prefixed, with every parameter set of `Config` inserted before each keyframe (§3.1).
- **Chunk streams:** data on csid 5, audio on csid 4, video on csid 6; message stream ID 1. A type-3 header is never used for a new message directly after a type-0 header on the same csid.
- **Paths:** a publisher's path (`rtmp.NormalizePath`) is the publish key and stays secret. A player's path is `<app>/<stream name>`, where app is given once, to `RegisterPlay` (osdev flag `-rtmp-play-app`, default `live`) and the stream name is the server's name for the stream (osdev: its `-stream name=`), independent of any publish path. A play request for a path that is a registered publish route is rejected; osdev checks at startup each `-stream` name against its listen publish path and logs a warning when they are equal (that stream is then unplayable); this is documented. Unknown path, publish path, a stream that is not live (its hub holds no keyframe yet), and every cap are answered with the same `NetStream.Play.StreamNotFound` "stream not available".
- **Bounds (`rtmpsrv`):** M2b's pre-publish caps (64 per listener, 4 per IP, IPv6 by /64) cover every session until it publishes or plays, and pre-publish chunk limits stay in force on play conns; at most 256 players per stream, 1024 per listener, 64 per IP (established); the per-listener values are a `Policy` the first registrant sets.
- **Push-out:** reconnect with exponential backoff 1 s → 30 s (reset after 30 s of successful sending); target userinfo and stream key redacted everywhere (keep 2 characters of the key) via a small shared `internal/redact` package (moved out of `internal/ingest`, which keeps using it).
- **Queues:** play and push subscribe with a queue of `max(256, cached frames + 64)` (`hub.SubscribeCached`) so the GOP replay always fits; a write that misses `WriteTimeout` ends the player or the push connection.

1. **Interop of the command exchange with each real peer** (ffmpeg play and ffmpeg `-listen` receive, MediaMTX pulling our play and receiving our publish, an ingest that expects `@setDataFrame` and echoed transaction IDs). 2. **A player that joins mid-GOP or falls behind** (throttled socket; a player that stops reading). Expected: starts at a keyframe with metadata and headers; after loss resumes at the next keyframe with headers and aligned audio; never a delta frame after a gap; a stalled player is dropped by the write deadline without slowing other players or the hub. Tests: `TestPlayJoinsAtKeyframe`, `TestPlaySlowConsumer`, `TestPlayStalledPlayerDropped` (Task 8).
3. **Stream keys and the shared port** (a viewer trying the publish path; unknown names; a flood of half-open sessions; one IP opening many players). Expected: publish keys never playable, identical answers, caps enforced. Tests: `TestPlayPathIsNotPublishKey`, `TestPlayUnknownPath`, `TestPlayFloodCaps` (Task 7).
4. **Quiet and broken links** (a 64 kbit/s push where the server only acks per window; a target that refuses, rejects, or closes mid-stream). Expected: a quiet healthy push is never ended by an idle timeout; failures back off 1→30 s and resume at a keyframe with headers; the hub is never blocked; the key never leaks. Tests: `TestPublishQuietServerNotIdle` (Task 4), `TestPushReconnects`, `TestPushRejectedBackoff`, `TestPushRedactsKey` (Task 9).
5. **Epoch changes, B-frames and long runs** (failover with a resolution change; a codec change; b-pyramid; a player joining after 5 h of uptime). Expected: one metadata+headers resend per epoch; codec change ends/republishes; composition time exact; per-connection timestamps start near 0. Tests: `TestMuxEpochResendsHeaders`, `TestMuxBFrames`, `TestMuxRebasePerConnection` (Task 6), `TestPlayCodecChangeEndsSession` (Task 8), `TestPushCodecChangeRepublishes` (Task 9), `TestM3b1EpochChange` (Task 11).
